## Progressive evolution without arbitrary damage rates

Use an energy/strength budget, not `integrity -= damagePerSecond * dt` tuned against successful scenarios.

1. For a resolved region, integrate absorbed power, radiative loss and conduction between TPS and substrate using finite heat capacity: `dE/dt = qAbsorbed*A - qRadiated*A - conductance*(Tsurface-Tsubstrate)`. Obtain heat capacity, density, conductivity/emissivity and temperature-dependent strength from verified material sources or an explicitly named sourced surrogate. Geometry/path length/attachment section assumptions must be recorded separately. Do not invent Starship-specific certified properties from the word “stainless”.
2. Derive component demand from existing physical forces and their arms: fin aerodynamic force/hinge moment, actuator demand, engine thrust load and structural load carried by an attached body. Convert demand to stress only where a declared section/lever geometry exists. Global felt g is a safety proxy, not a local hinge stress tensor.
3. Compare demand with remaining temperature-dependent capacity. Irreversible plastic work/remaining section loss requires a sourced constitutive surrogate; elastic utilization below yield does not automatically consume integrity. If no defensible ablation, creep or fatigue law exists, do not simulate that mechanism. A TPS breach can change substrate heating through its coverage/conductance once there is a justified attachment/coverage-loss mechanism.
4. Engine failure is deterministic at a modeled capacity breach and stops thrust/flow/countdown. Cascade occurs only through a declared physical connection and transferred energy/load; no automatic neighbour countdown or distance-only probability. A pressure-driven tank rupture requires tank pressure/wall data or a declared sourced pressure surrogate. Until available, support thermal/attachment failure and bounded leakage with honest assumptions, not fictional pressurization telemetry.

**Keep the existing instantaneous terminal predicates unchanged as conservative independent hard stops.** Finite temperatures supplement the equilibrium safety reading; they do not replace it with a cooler delayed state to survive above 1,533 K. Progression below the global stops is possible when local stress/thermal soak exceeds a smaller component's sourced remaining capacity. It is not guaranteed for every pristine nominal flight and must not be forced by lowering capacity until a dramatic precursor appears.

Initial damage injection is useful for truth/control/editor tests, but cannot stand in for progressive simulation. Require at least one deterministic exposure sequence, sourced/surrogate parameters frozen before flight evaluation, that causes measurable component evolution and subsequent force/control change while the original global hard limits remain below terminal values. If that sequence cannot be justified from available data, the constitutive model is an unresolved prerequisite, not a finished damage feature.

### Bounded first mechanism: thermally weakened fin root

Implement one source-supported mechanism before generalizing it to engines and tanks. A two-node TPS/root thermal model supplies root temperature; existing aerodynamic force supplies root bending demand. For a declared rectangular surrogate attachment, section modulus is `Z = b*t²/6` (m³), second moment is `Isection = b*t³/12` (m⁴), root stress is `abs(Mroot)/Z` (Pa), and utilization is stress divided by sourced yield strength at root temperature. With assumed cantilever length `L`, angular compliance is proportional to `Mroot*L/(E(T)*Isection)` (rad); derive the exact coefficient from the declared beam/load geometry. This is not the hull's vehicle moment of inertia.

Progressive thermal soak reduces the sourced modulus/strength and therefore changes delivered loaded fin angle/control effectiveness continuously before a discrete capacity breach. Solve aerodynamic moment and elastic angle consistently within a small fixed iteration bound; test sign, residual and convergence rather than assume one pass remains valid. Do not reduce aerodynamic area for mere elastic softening. Keep actuator slew/range limits; compliance can reduce achieved command, not increase hardware authority.

If sourced stress-strain/fracture-energy data justify a plastic-work law, stored irreversible root damage can derive from plastic work per remaining section volume, with all dimensions shown and failure energy fixed from that source. Otherwise **do not manufacture irreversible section loss**: retain continuous finite thermal soak/elastic capacity degradation and a discrete surrogate attachment failure at its declared capacity. Describe temperature-dependent capacity as condition, not permanent material erosion. This narrower first mechanism is progressive flight-affecting damage, but it does not claim validated fatigue, ablation or crack propagation. Subsequent extension to irreversible loss depends on its own material evidence.

Required source inputs are primary thermal-property records/coupon measurements for the explicitly chosen TPS surrogate (density, heat capacity, conductivity, emissivity), primary elevated-temperature modulus/yield/strain data for the declared steel or attachment surrogate, and a cited mechanical beam/load derivation. NASA TPSX material records are a research starting point, not proof that V3 uses a particular tile. Attachment section and conductance path geometry remain declared engineering assumptions where public hardware data do not exist. Freeze the assumption ledger before exposure/flight evaluation; prohibit selecting section dimensions or failure energy to force nominal scenario survival or dramatic detachment. Engine-health throttling cannot be inferred from the fin-root surrogate; use a separate sourced propulsion mechanism or complete capacity-triggered shutoff.

## Primary-source supplement — minimum thermal/structural surrogate

**Adopt finite thermal soak plus continuous elastic softening, followed by irreversible loss of a surrogate attachment at its declared usable capacity. Do not implement permanent plastic strain, fatigue, ablation, tank leaks or engine cascades from the evidence verified here.** This provides real preterminal control degradation without a damage-per-second knob. Calling detachment a conservative modeled attachment failure is essential: 304 stainless is not established to fracture brittlely at its proof stress.

Source check began at `2026-10-03 20:02:30 UTC`. Primary-source searches found the following usable inputs and limits.

### Thermal properties: verified

[NIST, Thermal Performance of Fire Resistive Materials, §3.1.1, printed p7/PDF p15](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=860705) supplies 304 density `7920 kg/m³`, `cp(T)=6.683+0.04906*T+80.74*ln(T)` J/(kg K), and `k(T)=9.705+0.0176*T−1.60e−6*T²` W/(m K), with T in kelvin. Its heat-capacity figure spans 0–1200°C. Use these as 304-surrogate thermal properties, not certified V3 alloy data. Do not extrapolate NIST's separate cryogenic 304 fit beyond its approximately300K domain.

Own derivation: store specific enthalpy relative to293.15K, using the analytic integral `H(T)=6.683*T+0.02453*T²+80.74*(T*ln(T)−T)` minus its reference value. Then `m*[H(Tnext)−H(T)] = netPower*dt`. Invert monotonically with a fixed bounded solve. This avoids treating variable heat capacity as a constant or adding a tuned heating time constant. Limit use to293.15–1473.15K; initialization below room temperature requires a separately matched cryogenic model or explicit room-temperature surrogate start.
